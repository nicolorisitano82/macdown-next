Things you have learned to live with

Not a list of features: a list of small daily annoyances, and what happens here instead.

The usual

You compare two versions of a note, somebody re-wrapped the paragraphs, and every line is marked as changed. You give up and read both.

Here

Compare by paragraph and the same pair gives nine rows instead of a hundred and eighty-one — with the words that differ marked inside the row, and a mark in the margin for people who do not separate red from green.

The usual

A diagram means leaving the document: another app, an export, a picture pasted back in that nobody can edit.

Here

Describe the flow in your own language and the model on your Mac writes the Mermaid — drawn beside the source before it goes in, and refused if it would not draw. It stays text you can edit.

The usual

An assistant that can help with your notes wants them uploaded first, and then it can do rather more than read them.

Here

One folder, declared by you, handed to an assistant over a pipe: search, read, list, outline, backlinks. No tool that writes unless you start it that way, none that deletes at all, and every call written into a log on your Mac.

The usual

Pressing space on a Markdown file in Finder shows you the source, with the fences and the pipes.

Here

It shows the document as it reads — headings, tables, to-do boxes, pictures, diagrams, formulas, a table of contents — from a page that has no scripts and no way to reach the network.

The usual

Something rewrites your file underneath — a branch change, a script, an agent — and the editor keeps showing a document that no longer exists, then saves over it.

Here

It notices. With nothing unsaved it reloads and keeps your place; with changes in hand it asks once; if the file has gone it says so, with the path. A version is kept before anything rewrites anything.

What it does

The short version. The guide has all of it, feature by feature, including what each one deliberately refuses to do.

The editor

It draws the Markdown

Markers disappear until the caret reaches them. Lists get a hanging indent, quotations a rule down the margin, headings room above. Tables are edited by command, and their columns are drawn in the preview, which has the room for them.

None of it touches the file: what you save is the string you typed, to the character.

Diagrams

Mermaid 11, Graphviz, draw.io

Mermaid 11.17 up from 8.4.3, Graphviz with six layout engines, and zoom and pan on any diagram — the zoom survives a refresh, so you keep your place while typing.

The draw.io importer turns every page of a .drawio file into a PNG beside the document, with 97 shape libraries inside the plug-in: it draws with no connection at all.

Exporting

Files that survive the trip

HTML, PDF, EPUB 3.3, and Word that arrives with its tables as tables, its lists indented and its code monospaced on Windows too — AppKit's own writer loses all three.

Diagrams are drawn into every export, rather than shipping the source and hoping the reader runs JavaScript.

Textbundle and Textpack as well — the Markdown as written with its pictures beside it, in somebody else's format on purpose — written and read alike.

In the Finder

Press space, read the document

A Quick Look extension renders .md files with the same style sheet the app opens with. Pictures beside the document travel with the preview; the page itself cannot reach the network.

Diagrams and formulas are drawn there too — mermaid, all six Graphviz engines, TeX through MathJax — and [[WikiLinks]] are links, marked when the page they point at is not there yet.

A panel in the preferences says whether macOS has it, which copy is providing it and which version — and installs, updates or removes it, because that registration goes wrong quietly.

The two panes

One selection, in both

Select words in the preview and the same words are selected in the source, with the focus there: delete removes them, typing replaces them. Select a sentence in the editor and the page marks it.

Which words, exactly, is the whole problem — the rendered text is not the source. The second test of a paragraph is the second one, and a selection that cannot be placed with certainty leaves the editor alone and says so.

Writing

A prose checker, and help that runs here

Qualifiers, weasel words, hedging, wordiness, passive tells and repeated words, in English and Italian, with a panel that lists them and takes you to each one.

Improve, correct, make formal, plain, shorter, longer — over a selection or a paragraph, from a model running on your Mac through llama.cpp. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Collecting

A folder you can navigate

[[Wiki links]] between neighbouring documents, and a list of which documents point at this one, with the line and the sentence around it.

Save a web page as Markdown beside your notes, with the address and the date at the top: a clipping without its source is a quotation without a source.

Drawing

Describe it; the model draws it

Right-click in the editor, say what the flow is in your own language, and the model on your Mac writes the Mermaid — labels in the language you wrote in. Nothing leaves the machine.

The source comes back editable, with the diagram drawn beside it as you type, and it can only go into the document once it has actually drawn: a diagram Mermaid refuses shows its complaint instead of landing in your notes.

Comparing

This document, beside another

⌃⌥⌘D puts the document next to any other file and marks what differs — the left side being what is in the editor, unsaved changes and all, because what did I change is asked before saving as often as after.

A changed line is one row carrying both versions, with the words that differ marked inside it. The columns keep their own line numbers, scroll together, and can fold away everything that is the same.

Importing

A Word file, read into the document

Somebody sends a .docx. The choice was to retype it or to keep a format you cannot read without the program that wrote it. File ▸ Import is the third way: headings, lists, tables, quotations, links and pictures come across as Markdown, at the insertion point, in one step of undo. OpenDocument (.odt) too.

Both formats are a zip of XML, so nothing here needs a library — and the styles are read, not guessed: a paragraph names its style in whatever language Word was in, Titolo1 or Überschrift1, and only the style sheet inside the file knows that means a heading.

What cannot come across — footnotes, comments, tracked changes — is listed in a panel rather than left for you to discover later.

Colour

What a word looks like, said in Markdown

Markdown says what a thing is, not how it looks, so it has no colour of its own. The two languages that grew out of it and do say it — Djot and Pandoc — spell it the same way, and so does this: [acceso]{style="color:#c00"}.

Select some words, right-click, Style ▸: the colour of the words, the colour behind them, four sizes. Click inside words that are already styled and the same menu changes them, starting from the colour that is already there.

In the editor the braces hide until the caret reaches them, the way a link's destination does, and the words are drawn in the colour they ask for. Only attributes that describe are let through to the page — an event handler stays on the page as you typed it, because the preview is a web view and the file is often somebody else's.

Agents

A folder an assistant can read

macdownext-mcp, inside the application, hands one declared folder to Claude Code, Claude Desktop or any other MCP client over a pipe: search it, read a file, list what is there, see a document's outline, ask who cites a document or which notes declare a field. The application does not have to be running.

Reading only, until you start it with --append or --write: then it can add to the end of a document, make one that is not there, and replace text that is. Nothing deletes, nothing renames, nothing writes over a file that exists; a path that leaves the folder is refused; a second folder is an error rather than a second permission, and every call goes into a log on your Mac — which Settings ▸ Agents shows you, next to the line to paste into the client and the switch that turns the whole thing off.

What changed, concretely

This fork exists because of specific things that did not work. These are five of them.

Before

Graphviz replaced the whole page body with the first graph it drew, taking the rest of the document with it, and stopped redrawing after the first keystroke.

Now

Six layout engines, each its own fence language, redrawn as you type, zoomable, and following the system appearance.

Before

A Word export dropped the pictures, flattened tables into tab-separated paragraphs, lost list indentation, and named a Mac-only font with nothing to substitute it.

Now

Each of those is repaired in the file afterwards, and remote pictures are fetched into the package first, so the document opens whole on somebody else's computer.

Before

Finder showed the source of a Markdown file — hashes, asterisks and all — and there was no way to know whether a preview extension was even registered.

Now

Space renders the document — diagrams and formulas drawn, WikiLinks as links — and a preferences pane says where the extension stands and fixes it in one click.

Before

The preview was a page you could only read: selecting a sentence in it told the editor nothing, and finding those words in the source was your problem.

Now

The two panes share one selection, either way round. Delete on a selection made in the preview takes those words out of the source; a selection that cannot be placed with certainty leaves the editor alone and says so.

Before

A Word file arriving in your inbox meant retyping it, or keeping a format you cannot read without the program that wrote it.

Now

File ▸ Import reads a .docx or an .odt into the document where the cursor is, with its headings, lists, tables and pictures — and says what it could not carry over.

Before

A new version existed only if you went looking for it, and the editor coloured ##Titolo as a heading while the preview drew it as a paragraph.

Now

The app looks once a day and asks three times before doing anything, and both parsers agree about what a heading is — with a one-click fix when the space is missing.
